Company History

ASC, orignally called ACA, was founded in 1873. ACA stood for Alerting Communicators of America, and changed to American Signal Corporation when they went backrupt and were bought and reformed as ASC. Their headquarters is in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. They are currently still a private company.

Alertronic

The Alertronic was made from 1983 to 2011 and was produced by ACA which got carried over into ASC. It could produce 108 to 128 dB at 100 feet. It was ACA'S first fully electronic siren. There were many entries in this series of sirens. The Quadren was introduced in 1992 and was considered an entry into the Alertonic series when it was introduced, but it was later renamed into the i-FORCE. It will have its own section apart from the Alertronic section.

Today, these sirens are rare because of poor sales and how old they are. Few rotational Alertronics actually remain in service.

All Alertronic sirens run on DC power and have battery-capability. Their batteries can be charged with solar power as an option. They can make ten signals with thirteen tones able to be programmed. They are also voice-capable.

The AL1000 was a directional siren which reaches 108 dB at 100 feet. It was made of fiberglass horns attached to an aluminum housing.

Here is a photo of the AL1000 and 2000

The AL2000 is also directional and is similar to the 1000 visually, except it has double the drivers on the inside, with two drivers per horn. It reached 112 dB at 100 feet.

The AL4000 was yet another directional addition to the series and was basically a 1000 or 200 doubled up. It had eight horns made of fiberglass and attached to an aluminum housing. Unlike the previous two, the housing is cubical rather than rectangular. It was also available without the square housing. It looked similar to a Whelen WS 2000. It had two drivers attached to each horn with two horns on each side of the housing. It could reach 116 dB at 100 feet.

With the AL6000, it was a bit different than the previous three, It was still directional, but it has 12 fiberglass horns attached to a rectangular housing with three horns per side. It had two drivers per horn and could make 121 dB at 100 feet.

The AL8000 was the largest directional Alertronic and was like the 6000, but with four horns per side instead of three, and like two 4000s on top of eachother. It has the same housing as the 6000 and again, two drivers per horn. Some sides and horns were removable with allowed for more configurations. It could reach 126 dB at 100 feet.

Little is known about the AL7200 siren. It was omnidirectional and was relatively short lived compared to the other sirens in the series. It had three rows of eight horns attached to an octagonal housing, which is certainly a new shape for these sirens. It is assumed to have two drivers per horn. There was a rumored AL12000 to exist, which would have had 30 speakers, but that is unknown and no evidence has been found of it. There is no decibel rating for this siren, and it seems like it was custom made for the Oak Ridge TN region because none have been found outside of that region.

The AL5000 was rotational that looked very unique. Because of its design, it could possibly fall over during high winds and wind damage. It was never very popular. It could produce 125 dB at 100 feet.

Similar to the AL5000, the AL6000R was rotational. Its design was similar to the Whelen WPS 4000, WS 3000, and Vortex. Whelen even considered suing for this, but decided not to because ACA was not as big of a competitor to them at the time. It could reach 125 dB at 100 feet.

The AR1600 or RE1600 was a rotational siren as a improved version of the AL6000R. The siren's aluminum parts were replaced with fiberglass and made shorter. It could reach 128 dB at 100 feet. It would later be named the RE1600 when ACA got acquired and changed their name to ASC. This siren was also so similar to the Whelen WPS4004 that they considered suing, but like the previous siren, they also decided that it simply was not worth it due to ACA not being a big enough competitor.

i-FORCE

Originally known as the Quadren, the i-FORCE was first released in 1992 and then changed to its present form in 2005 and is still being produced today. It is omnidirectional and was made to compete with the Federal Signal Modulator and the Whelen WPS2800 and 2900. It was not as popular as those sirens, though. It was part of the Alertronic line of sirens, but it reaally did not fit with them. It was renamed in 1994, but there is also a present version of the i-FORCE, as it got redesigned. It can also produce 113 to 128 dB at 100 feet.

Here is the only existing photo evidence of a Quadren cut in half, meant to be mounted on walls.

The original version of this siren (and the present version as well) is made of stacked cell speakers. It looks very similar to the FS Modulator. The sound in these cells are projected out of the top and bottom of each cell, with every other cell being used to disperse the sound and project it outward. These cells are made of fiberglass. It was designed to were it can be cut in half or in quarters to be mounted on walls, but none of those wall-mounted sirens are known to have been installed or exist today. That did help their versatility in theory, though.

The Quadren sirens were named by habing Q followed by the wattage of the siren. The original version is very rare because it did not sell well. There are around four left in existance, with only one, in Thailand, being still active to this day.

The i-FORCE in its present form would project sound downward instead of coming from both the top and bottom. The desing was simplified, making it less costly to make. These sirens came in one, two, three, or four active cells, with a dummy cell at the bottom to disperse sound. They were named by having STi or i-FORCE followed by the number of drivers in the hundreds or thousands place. For example, a siren with eight drivers would be the STi/i-FORCE 800, and the 24 driver siren would be named the STi/i-FORCE 2400.

E-Class

This siren was directional, being made up of speaker arrays. It was first made in 2007 and is still being made today. It was made as a cheaper alternative to the i-FORCE and can produce 109 to 126 dB at 100 feet. Each siren can cam swivel up to 45 degrees in any direction depending on how they are mounted. Each array is mounted to a rectangular frame that changes height depending on how many speakers are mounted.

The naming scheme is EC followed by how many speakers there are. For example, a five speaker siren would be the EC5. This siren has battery backups and voice capability. They are not as common, but they are very effective.

Clarity

This siren is ASC's newest siren, with it beginning production in 2020. It is small and able to be shipped though standard mail, weighing less than 30 lbs. It is made for office parks and campuses and has voice capability. It can easily be serviced, but it cannot produce as much sound as an actually effective siren can. Very few models have been sold as of this year.

Screamer

This series of sirens are small and omnidirectional. They were later produced under ASC as the OM series. It was made from 1967 to 2004 and produces 101 to 112 dB at 100 feet. The early models has the same shroud shape and were very uniform in design. The naming scheme was different, being named the Model 100, 105 and 108. Three known early models were produced.

This here is an S5.

In 1973, they got redesigned. They became the S2, S3, S5, S7.5, and S10, being named after its horsepower. The 2, 5, and 7.5 were carried over to ASC with the paintjob changing from yellow to gray. The 10 variant was discontinued in 1994 and the other sirens were renamed, becoming the OM102, 0M109, and OM112. The shape of these sirens' shroud would be different depending on the model. It was very low-maintenance. The S2 variant was the smallest made, whils the S3 was short-lived.

Banshee

The left siren is a 115, the middle is the Performance Plus, and the right is the Super

This siren was included in the OM series but is its own unique siren. It was made from 1968 to 2004 and produces 112 to 120 dB at 100 feet. It was released alongside the Screamer. There were two models, with one being single-rotor and the other being a double and much larger. The 110, the single-rotor, had its motor uncovered at the top, while the 115, the larger one, had a motor cover, with one unit currently existing in Ohio. There was also a Performance Plus version with had battery backups and DC power. There was another version called the SuperBanshee wish was very very big and dual-rotor. It is the loudest of the AC-powered Banshees. It was not popular, though.

Cyclone

This siren was made from 1968 to 2007 and was omnidirectional. It has a unique conical shape, which sets it apart from other sirens. It produces 125 dB at 100 feet, which is very impressive for an omnidirectional siren. There was a 125 version made in 1980 which was an improved version of the original Cyclone 120.

Penetrator

The left siren is the P10 and the right is the P50

This siren was made with four models, the P10, P15, PN20, and P50. They are rotational sirens made from 1980 to 2007 and can produce 125 to 135 dB at 100 feet. Many can be found in service today and is one of ACA's most popular sirens. Due to time constaints, each model cannot be fully described, but they essentially were very similar.
