History

Whelen Engineering Company was founded in Connecticut in 1952. It is a private comany currently headquartered in Chester, Connecticut. Whelen has four divisions, but the Mass Notification Products Division is the one that concerns tornado sirens. There is not much information on the company's history.

WPS 4000 and Vortex

The Whelen WPS 4000 and Vortex are very similar sirens. They look pretty much the same. They have been produced since 1988, and the WPS 4008 variant from 1988 to 2003. These sirens range from 118 to 133 dB at 100 feet, which is quite impressive. These sirens are Whelen's most popular series of rotating electronic sirens. They are curremtly their only rotating electronic siren that they are selling. Currently, they are selling the WPS 4003, WPS 4004, VORTEXR1, VORTEXR2, VORTEXR3, and VORTEXR4. The WPS 4004 and the VORTEXR4 are their most popular sirens in the series. They are very popular, replacing aging sirens, but they are not as reliable when not maintained properly. The difference between the WPS 4000 series and the Vortex series is that the Vortex is voiceless, and the WPS 4000 series can do voice messages.

In 1990, Whelen introduced the WPS 4000-4 and WPS 4000-3. Basically, they were a WPS 4000 cut in half. Other than that, they are pretty much the same. Some Vortex models had a fin installed in the back to help it rotate during high winds, but only earlier units had it.

Here is a VortexR4 finback. The fin is very prominent here. Compare that to the Vortex without the fin on the right, taken from the Whelen website.

In 1999 or 2000, the names of the sirens were changed. What was the WPS 4000-3 was now the WPS 4003. That name change applied to all WPS 4000 series sirens. And around 2002, the Vortex sirens were renamed to the VortexR series. The WPS 4008 was discontinued in 2003 because of lack of sales and production costs.

Hornet

The Whelen Hornet is a rotational siren that has been manufactured since 1996. It produces 118 dB at 100 feet. It is designed to be a cheaper alternative to the WPS 4000 and Vortex series. The Vortex was marketed as pretty cheap, but some towns simply could not afford it. Luckily, the Hornet was marketed as a super duper low cost alternative. It is often used at fire stations.

There is also a second version of the Hornet, called the WS400H. It is directional and made to be used as part of a speaker array or just as a standalone speaker. It can be mounted on poles or on walls. It is very rare, and only few still exist. Most have been removed or destroyed. They were not well-advertised by Whelen. This siren still remains in production, even though there isn't much of a demand for it.

On the left is a photo of the Hornet. On the right is a photo of the WS400H mounted on a wall. They look the same, it's just the fact that the Hornet is rotational while the WS400H is made to be directional.

WS 1000

This siren was Whelen's first siren in the outdoor mass notification market. It was made foor only two years, from 1977 to 1979. It would later be improved upton, becoming the WS 2000. The WS 1000 was very basic, being just a directional speaker array. Sure, it was revolutionary for its time because it was electronic, but nowadays, much better exists. It did not sell very well then. Very few confirmed WS 1000 sirens exist to this day.

It could produce 112 dB at 100 feet, but it is unknown whether that is for the 4 horn or 8 horn model. This siren had voice capabilities and could do three signals.

This is an ad from Whelen in 1978. It shows the WS 1000 and its controller box.

WS 2000

This siren, later renamed to be WPS 2000, were directional speaker arrays made from 1979 to 1990. They were an improvement upon their previous siren, the WS 1000. The WS 1000 was discontinued in order to make way for the 2000. It was a significant improvement upon the previous version. It would later be replaced by the WPS 2700 and the WPS 2800 series which were omnidirectional.

Nowadays, these sirens are rare. The drivers can suffer from driver failure because they are over 30 years old, and some places have replaced these aging systems. Each array could have two, three, or four horns. Up to five speaker arrays could be on one controller. There were four different subtypes of WS 2000 sirens. the WS 2000-109 had two speakers per array and would produce 109 db at 100 feet. the WS 2000-112 had three speakers per array and could produce 112 dB at 100 feet. The last two both had four horns per array, being the WS 2000-115 and the WS 2000-20. The only difference was the number of drivers. The 115 had 16 drivers, and the 20 had 20 drivers. They both produced 115 dB at 100 feet. These sirens would later be renamed in the early 80's, being named after how many speakers were in each unit. They were named he WS 2000-8, 2000-12, 2000-16, and 2000-20. The names would change once again as in 1987, all sirens would change their WS prefix to WPS.

Pictured here is a WS 2000-12. As you can see, it has three speakers per array, with four arrays.

There was a rotational version introduced, called the WS 2000R around 1981. It had one speaker array with four horns. It was discontinued pretty soon because of technical issues over time, sometime in the early to mid 80s.

Here is a 2000R. It has one speaker array and is rotational.

The Whelen WS-2500 had 20 speakers and was conceptualized in 1981. It would have been Whelen's first true 360 degree omindirectional siren, had it been produced. It only exists in advertisements and would later be adapted as the WS 2000-20.

WPS 2700

This siren is made of siren cells stacked on top of eachother, much like the Federal Signal Modulator. It was revolutionary for its time, being the first multicellular electronic siren. It was produced from 1987 to 1991. It was omnidirectional and could produce 109-121 dB at 100 feet. It would later be replaced by the WPS 2800 which improved on its design and allowed for more cells to be stacked on top. Many WPS 2700 sirens can be found today.

This siren's design came from the WS 2500, which was never produced. Sound was projected in 360 degrees, covering the full radius. It had voice capability, a battery backup, and solar capabilities, which helped it be resilient.

It had quite an odd naming scheme, having the number of cells be in the tens column instead of the ones. So for example, a WPS 2700 with three cells would be called the WPS 2730. The 2720 could produce 109 dB at 100 feet, the 2730 with 112 dB at 100 feet, the 2740 with 115 dB at 100 feet, the 2750 with 118 dB at 100 feet, the 2760 with 119 dB at 100 feet, and the 2790 with 121 dB at 100 feet.

WPS 2800

The 2800 was another siren made from stacked speaker cells. It was produced from 1991 to 2006 and improved upon the 2700 as its sucessor. For one, the cell supports were not made of aluminum any more. There were also mesh screens to keep debris out of the cells. The cells were also made larger, which made it easier to tell apart from the 2700. They are still common to find because of how reliable they are, and becaus the older controllers have been replaced, keeping the siren.

The naming system has been changed. Instead of the number of cells being in the tens column, it was moved to the ones. So for example, a 2800 with eight cells would be called the 2808, and six would be called the 2806. These sirens could hold one to ten cells, which was an improvement from the previous series. The 2801 could produce 108 dB at 100 feet, the 2802 with 114 dB at 100 feet, the 2803 with 115 dB at 100 feet, the 2804 with 118 dB at 100 feet, the 2805 with 119 dB at 100 feet, the 2806 with 121 dB at 100 feet, the 2807 with 123 dB at 100 feet, the 2808 with 124 dB at 100 feet, the 2809 at 125 dB at 100 feet, and the 2810 with 126 dB at 100 feet.

There was a series called the WS-280 series that was introduced in 1998. They were designed for fire stations to be activated locally, which would reduce costs. They could only have one to five cells. Other than that, they are basically the same as the 2800 series. The naming conventions were the same, being the number of cells in the ones column. This series was made until 2002, being made for a very short amount of time.

WPS 2900

This was yet another series of modular speaker cell sirens made by Whelen. They have been made since 2006 and it is their most popular omnidirectional siren. It was made to improve upon the 2800. One of the improvements was making the drivers easier to remove and replaced from the side of the siren. which helps to lengthen the siren's lifespan. Other than that, it was pretty much the same as the 2800 and 2700.

The 2901 can produce 109 dB at 100 feet, the 2902 with 115 dB at 100 feet, the 2903 with 119 dB at 100 feet, the 2904 with 121 dB at 100 feet, the 2905 with 123 dB at 100 feet, the 2906 with 125 dB at 100 feet, the 2907 with 126 dB at 100 feet, the 2908 with 127 dB at 100 feet, the 2909 with 128 dB at 100 feet, and the 2910 with 190 dB at 100 feet. These sirens kept the naming conventions of the 2800, which is nice.

WS 3000

The WS/WPS 3000 was made for ten years; it was made from 1981 to 1991. It could produce 124 db at 100 feet. It ended up being replaced by the 4000 later on. Even though they are now discontinued, many WS 3000 sirens still operate. There is not much that really distinguishes them from their successor, the 4000.
