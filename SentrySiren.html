Company History

Sentry Siren was founded in 1972. Their headquarters is in Penrose Colorado since 2020 after relocating from Canon City. They were formally the Sterling Siren Fire Alarm Corporation. Today, Sentry Siren makes the largest mechanical omnidirectional sirens.

3V8, 3V8H, 3V8HB, and 3V8HC

The 3V8 is a single-rotor omnidirectional siren. It has been produced since 1980 and continues to be produced to this day. It is the smallest and cheapest Sentry Siren model. It produces 107-109 dB at 100 feet and is 5 horsepower. On the top of the siren, there is a rain shield to prevent water from accumilating. This siren is easy to maintain and insures that it will last a long time. There are three more variants of this siren that this entry will cover. To sum up the 3V8, it is very simple, cheap, and small, but it works well.

The 3V8H variant had eight horns added to the siren. the H at the end means there are horns. Pretty simple! It is slightly more expensive but is also slightly louder, at 109 dB at 100 feet. It was introduced as early as 1999. Other than the horns, it is just a 3V8.

Left is the 3V8. Right is the 3V8H. Very clearly, the H model has horns.

Another variant is th 3V8HB with is a DC powered variant. It can run on battery backups for around 30 minutes. As the name implies, it comes with the horns as standard.

This here is a photo of the special C variant from the Sentry Siren website back in 2003.

The final variant is the 3V8HC which was a custom made siren for the Centennial Correctional Facility in Canon City, Colorado in 2003. It was made because the concrete walls of the facility would have dampened the sound, and Sentry Siren's other sirens would simply not do. They required a specialized siren to cut through the walls' dampening. The 3V8HC has pointed horns that disperse sound out and down to penetrate the concrete walls.

7V8 and 7V8B

The 7V8 is an omnidirectional siren that has been manufactured since 2000. It produces 112 dB at 100 feet. It comes with horns as a standard. It, as all Sentry Siren's single-rotor sirens, they're quite simple in design.

Here is a 7V8B. It looks the exact same as a 7V8.

The 7V8B was made in 2002 as a DC-powered version of the 7V8 with a battery backup. Other than that, it is pretty much the same as the 7V8.

10V

This siren is yet another omnidirectional siren, being introduced in 1980. It is still being made to this day. It produces 115 dB at 100 feet. At the time of its release, it was Sentry's largest single-motor siren. There is a B variant which has battery backup capabilities. It is a special order, though. There is also a corrosion-resistant stainless steel version called the 10VSS for places with salty air that would rapidly corrode the siren. A fiberglass version called the 10VF is also a variant. Earlier models were 7.5 horsepower, but they are 10 horsepower most of the time.

Pictured above is a stainless steel 10V from Sentry Siren's website back in 2004.

14V

This siren was introduced in 2015 and still is being made today. It is yet another omnidirectional siren. It stands out, though, because it is the largest of Sentry Siren's single rotor sirens. It replaced the 10V as the largest single rotor siren. It can produce 127 dB at 100 feet and is 14 or 15 horsepower.

There is also a B variant which can use a battery backup and runs on DC power. It is, again, basically the same thing as the non B version if you exclude those details.

10V1T

The 10V1T is a vertical dual rotor siren produced since 2022. It is very new for a torado siren. It produces 120 dB at 100 feet, or so it is claimed. It had yet to be validated. This siren is also 10 horsepower. It is the smallest and cheapest dual rotor siren from Sentry Siren.

This photo was taken from the official Sentry Siren website.

Released a few months after the 10V2T was discontinued in June, the 10V1T did not appear on Sentry Siren's webstie until early 2023. It is bascially two 3V8H sirens on top of each other. It is only available in single-tone. As of now, it is only available using AC power. Sentry Siren states they don't intend to make a DC-powered version, but that may change.

15V2T

Yet another dual-tone siren, it is actually Sentry's most popular of the dual-tones. It began production in 1986 and still is in production. It makes 124 dB of sound at 100 feet, which is pretty good. This siren is also 15 or 16 horsepower. The 15V2T has 16 horns. The units made after the late 2000s have pointed horns to keep debris out, while older models have flat ends.

The B variant was made in 2002 and was yet again another DC-powered, battery-backup-capable version of the original siren. With this siren, the two motors don't run at the same speed, which makes the two sounds clash. It harms the siren's range, but it is definitely more distinct.

16V1TB

This siren comes as DC-powered and battery-backup-capable by default. It can produce 129 dB at 100 feet, and has been produced since 2004.

The older units had flat-ended horns, but they have pointed ends now to keep debris out. These sirens have two ports per horn. This siren also has the distinct sount of the 15V2TB because of the DC power. This siren remains one of Sentry Siren's most popular sirens and is their second loudest siren as claimed on their website.

20V1T and 20V2T

There are two versions of this siren, being in production since 1980. The 1T is single-tone, while the 2T is dual-tone. They are the second highest horsepower siren that Sentry Siren has, at 20 horsepower. They are claimed to make 125 dB of sound at 100 feet, althought that has yet to be confirmed.

The 20V2T was the loudest, largest, and most expensive siren they had upon its release. It kept that staus until the late 1990s. When they began being produced, they were made with square horns. They were only made for one year, with only 50 units made before Sentry Siren concluded it was too difficult and expensive to make. They were changed back into the round horn shortly after. Very few of those square-horn models remain. They are no longer active.

Left is the rare square horn version. This photo was taken before it got destroyed by lightning, being no longer active. The right photo is the special order 25V2TSC back when it was on Sentry Siren's website.

In Portland, Colorado, there was a special variant called the 25V2TSC made specifically for the Holcim cement plant. It was made to be airtight so cement dust could not enter and damage the siren's insides. It was removed sometime between 2015 and 2021, which is quite a large gap. Nobody knows where they are now.

40V2T

Being the loudest siren by Sentry Siren and one of the loudest omnidirectional siren ever produced, this siren began production in the late 1990s. It produces 128 dB at 100 feet, even though Sentry's website claims it makes 131 dB. This siren has different rotors and stators than their other sirens. It uses 10 horns which are short but wide. The top row of horns has three ports per horn, and the bottom row has two ports per horn. Later models had pointed horns. It can come in a single-tone, but only one exists currently. It is very large and requires lots of power, so it isn't as popular.

The left photo is one of the only B variant models made. The right photo is at the Holcim plant, which is the special SC version. It has since been removed.

The B variant had battery capability and DC power. It never made it to production, as it would often overload its components during testing. The SC variant was another special order siren made for the Holcim clement plant. It was airtight so cement powder would not get in the siren, damaging it. Only one was ever built.

MacroBurst and MicroBurst

The Macro and MicroBurst sirens are intened for notifying small areas. They are not on the Sentry Siren website but are still sold today. They are very rare. The MicroBurst produces 106 dB at 10 feet. Both sirens have been made since 2025, so they are very new. There is not much currently known about these sirens.

5V

This siren was produced from 1980 to 2000 and was one of Sentry Siren's original sirens, being replaced by the 7V8. It could make 112 dB of sound at 100 feet and was 7.5 horsepower. Instead of horns, this siren has a skirt at the bottom to direct sound out and down.

There is a version that is rumored to exist, called the 7V. It has a taller motor housing than normal, but that is the only real evidence there is, as there is little to no evidence to support this. There is no documentation from Sentry Siren that this existed, so most likely, it is false.

10V2T

The 10V2T is a now discontinued siren made from around 1973 to 2022. It could produce 120 dB at 100 feet and was 10 horsepower. The reason why it was discontinued was beause its sales weren't as good as the other sirens. This siren was one of Sentry's earliest orignally designed sirens. It has two different horn sizes, but skirts can be added for special order. The early prototype lookes strange, but it would certainly have been beautiful if it were released that way.

On this siren, there are 16 horns, eight of which are of the shorter kind, for short distances. Many of these sirens are still operational, but that will change as the years go by and they age.

Left is the prototype, which is unique and absolutely beautiful. I wish it went into production so we could see a truly unique siren. The right image is the only SCS model.

There was a 3 signal version that was made for a very short time. Around five are known to still exist. There was also a SCS model made for a methane plant neaby Sentry Siren's headquarters. Only one was made and it is at the Magellan Pipleline Co. in Pleasant Hill, Iowa.

SS3 Defender

The Defender, also known as the Model SS3, was a siren produced for ten years, from 2001 to 2011. This was Sentry's first and last rotational siren. It could produce 127 dB at 100 feet and was 7.5 or 8 horsepower. It was first made because some towns requested a rotational siren from Sentry Siren. There was a contest for the name of this siren, in which the name "Defender" won. Early models were 8 horsepower. Their design was later updated. Its horn sort of makes a point at the top. It also has a rounded motor cover, similar to the Roundback variant of the Federal Signal 2001. It would later be updated to be square-shaped.

Here is a photo of a First Generation Defender. Notice the rounded motor cover and the pointed top of the horn

There was a reason why Sentry Siren has not made any rotational sirens since. It had many, many issues which caused it to be very rare to find today. Some issues include:

  • First Generation's rotator would suffer rapid wear from lack of lubrication if not maintained regularly and well enough
  • The siren would shake a lot and stutter as it rotated if not regularly maintained due to the above issue
  • The batteries in the siren would run out if not regularly replaced which would cause it to fail completely sometimes
  • Rotational mechanism was complex compared to other rotational sirens
  • They were very vulnurable to corrosion
  • Their paint flakes off and further allowed corrosion to damage the siren
  • Lack of popularity and reliability
  • Often got replaced in costal areas due to corrosion

There were more than enough problems to dissuade Sentry Siren from making another rotational siren. Today, they are very rare to find in service or just at all.

VR

The Model VR was Sentry Siren's first attempt at making an electronic siren. It was made for only two years, being from 2018 to 2020, which is fairly recent. It was claimed to make 120 dB at 100 feet. It had voice capability but it had a very high pitched tone which was ineffective for mass notification. It also was not well-marketed. Only a few exist today.

Each siren is made of around 5 to 30 speakers in arrays with 1 to 6 speakers per direction with five different directions. It had a very high wattage for even the smallest model.
